Elbow and wrist always move together through a single value, fold. In a real wing a linkage (radius and ulna sliding against each other) bends the wrist whenever the elbow bends, so one value is anatomically right as well as simple. That leaves each wing with five values to drive: flap, sweep, twist, fold and alula. The primary fan and slots follow from those values and the airspeed.

5 · Feather plan

GroupCountAttached toBehaviour
Primaries p1–p1010hand (p1–p6 on the carpometacarpus, p7–p10 on the major digit)Fan from almost aft (p1) to almost spanwise (p10). They close as the wrist folds. On slotted species the emarginated outer feathers splay apart in slow flight.
Secondaries9–14ulna (one per quill knob)Point aft. They stack along the forearm as the elbow folds. A longer arm wing carries more of them (hawk 14, falcon 10).
Tertials3end of the humerusFill the gap between the secondaries and the body.
Greater, median and lesser covertsrowsover each flight feather's baseFollow their feather. They smooth the top of the wing and hide the quills. The lesser coverts form a band behind the leading edge.
Alula3digit I at the wristDeploys forward and up as a slat at high angle of attack. It's the visual cue for slow flight.
Scapulars≈6shoulderCover the wing root, most visible when folded.
Rectrices (tail)12pygostyleFan, pitch and twist as a group. Species differ in tail shape (§6).

Build: a Skeleton3D made in code from each species' proportions. Each feather is a shared tapered mesh, placed per bone with its own length, width and notch. Coverts are a few skinned strips. That comes to about 70 feathers per bird, drawn as one MultiMesh per bird and updated from the bone poses each frame. A test can check that the rig's drawn wing area matches the flight model's wing_area, which keeps the visuals honest to the physics. The plates below already do that check.

6 · Wing shape by species

Yes: the shape changes a lot from species to species, mostly in four places:

  • The arm-to-hand length ratio.
  • Which primary is longest, which sets a pointed or rounded tip.
  • How many outer primaries are emarginated (slotted).
  • Chord and tail shape.
